**FINAL MASTER RULE FOR PROGRAMMER**

Use a clean, readable final report format. The report does not need to copy the exact visual style or formatting of the Hazelwood run sheet. The important thing is that the information be grouped in the familiar workflow order so the report is easy to review, easy to compare against the source documents, easy to cut and paste from, and free of unnecessary clutter.

Use this practical report section order: ORDER INFORMATION, CHAIN OF TITLE, MORTGAGES / DEEDS OF TRUST, JUDGMENTS / LIENS, MISCELLANEOUS DOCUMENTS, LEGAL DESCRIPTION, ADDITIONAL INFORMATION, and NAMES SEARCHED.

The PDFs are usually compiled in this practical working order: request sheet/order information if provided; assessor sheets, tax information, and order information supporting documents; deeds and chain of title documents, along with pertinent supporting documents that appear within the chain of title; mortgages/deeds of trust, along with associated mortgage documents such as assignments, modifications, and substitute trustee documents; judgments/liens, if any; and miscellaneous documents such as outsales, agreements, leases, easements, C\&Rs, utility ROWs, and similar documents. Use the PDF compilation order to help identify what section each document belongs in, but place the extracted information into the correct report section.

Use ALL CAPS wherever practical for extracted report content, especially names, document titles, notes, short reference entries, and Names Searched entries. Preserve recorded names, legal descriptions, AKA/FKA language, misspellings, punctuation, abbreviations, and apparent errors exactly as recorded when the rule calls for exact indexing. Do not silently correct deed language, names, descriptions, spelling, punctuation, abbreviations, or apparent recording mistakes.

For the File Number field, use the PDF filename first if it contains a usable order number and that number matches or corresponds with the order/request information. Use the entire number exactly as shown. Never drop digits, shorten the number, break apart number groups that belong together, or invent an order number. If no usable order number is assigned by the company, use the company name and full property address. For naming the final output file, use the company name plus the full ID when both are available. If there is no usable ID, use the company name plus the full property address, or the full property address alone if no company is available.

Always leave space for the Effective Date field. Do not guess the Effective Date. Look for it on the front page of the PDF; when present, it may appear as a large, bold date that is noticeably bigger than the surrounding print. Use it only if it is clearly being used as the Effective Date. If the Effective Date is not clearly provided, leave it blank. Use the actual Completed Date when available, and otherwise leave it blank unless the user provides it.

Preserve Parcel IDs exactly, including leading zeroes, and keep multiple Parcel IDs in source order. Capture tax information exactly as shown, including every installment, paid date, due date, assessed value, land value, improvement value, and actual delinquent amount owed. Delinquent is not a yes/no field. If a tax ticket is unpaid, capture the original amount, the due date, and the full delinquent amount shown on the ticket, including penalties and fees if listed. Display multiple Parcel IDs, tax items, due dates, paid dates, and delinquent amounts stacked vertically when possible. Township should normally be the city from the property address. Do not display search depth. Excise tax may remain if available but is not required.

For Chain of Title, list entries from newest to oldest. Create separate numbered entries in order. Use short, familiar deed titles such as GENERAL WARRANTY, SPECIAL WARRANTY, QUITCLAIM, GIFT DEED, DEED OF ASSUMPTION, ESCHEAT DEED, PARTITION DEED, and TRUSTEE’S DEED. Consideration must come from the deed itself and should only be a numeric amount or LOVE AND AFFECTION; otherwise leave it blank. Show full numeric values with complete digits and proper comma placement. Show In/Out Sale as a Yes/No checkbox or equivalent Yes/No field. Mark YES when the current vesting owner takes in or sells out some of the land. Outsales are usually listed in Additional Information instead of as numbered Chain of Title entries.

Grantors and grantees must be listed exactly as written in the deed. Preserve AKA, FKA, FORMERLY KNOWN AS, and similar recorded name language. If a name appears as both grantor and grantee in the same instrument, list it in both places. Do not state marital status. Use an ampersand only when the instrument specifically shows parties as married; otherwise use commas. Always use full names.

Do not add notes or descriptions for every deed. Only add notes for special situations such as deceased-person references, wills, LOHs, REAs, life estates, divorce, third-party or fourth-party issues, foreclosure-related documents, or other special supporting documents. If a life estate is reserved, show the reserving grantor or grantors in the Grantee(s) line as RESERVES LIFE ESTATE or RESERVE LIFE ESTATE and show the other party or parties as REMAINDERMENT. If a deceased-person or probate issue appears, use the proper wording: WILL OF, LOH FOR, REA FOR, NO WILL OR LOH FOR, or REFERENCE MADE TO A WILL FOR BUT NONE WAS FOUND. Use Yes/No special-note fields when available and place the note information in the corresponding field. When notes are used, keep them in the same order they appear in the source documents.

For old deeds, look for the word TESTE. On very old deeds, the recording date is often found in the final paragraph immediately before the TESTE line and before the clerk’s signature.

For Trustee’s Deeds, index the Trustee’s Deed as the numbered Chain of Title entry and place related foreclosure documents under that entry as related or special-note items, not as separate numbered chain entries. Related documents may include ACCOUNT OF SALE, SUBSTITUTE TRUSTEES, MODIFICATION, FORECLOSED DOT, and other directly related foreclosure documents. If more than one DOT appears, identify the actual FORECLOSED DOT and any DOT OPEN AT THE TIME OF FORECLOSURE. Keep foreclosure-related supporting documents in the same order they appear in the packet. Resume numbered Chain of Title entries with the next actual deed or conveyance.

For Mortgages / Deeds of Trust, list open or reportable mortgages from oldest to newest. Use short-form abbreviations such as DOT, RFDT, and DTCL when appropriate. Associated documents usually include ASSIGNMENT, SUBSTITUTE TRUSTEE, and MODIFICATION, but allow OTHER when needed. Place associated mortgage documents under the mortgage they relate to whenever possible. Identify OPEN-ENDED or CLOSED-ENDED when the instrument language supports it. Open-ended instruments often contain FUTURE ADVANCE, FUTURE ADVANCEMENT, OPEN-ENDED, or similar language. Do not rely on maturity date alone.

Place Judgments / Liens after Mortgages / Deeds of Trust. Place Miscellaneous Documents after Judgments / Liens for supporting recorded documents needing fuller detail, including easements, utility ROWs, C\&Rs, leases, surveys, plats, estate documents, and other property-affecting instruments. Miscellaneous Documents and Additional Information are somewhat interchangeable depending on how much detail is needed. Additional Information is usually preferred for shorthand references, while Miscellaneous Documents should be used when fuller indexing is necessary.

Place Legal Description after Miscellaneous Documents for familiarity. The legal description should usually come from the vesting deed if readable. If the vesting deed legal description is not readable, incomplete, or unclear, use the best readable legal description from another reliable recorded source, such as a prior deed, later deed, deed of trust, mortgage attachment, or other recorded instrument clearly containing the property description. Preserve the legal description exactly as recorded, including misspellings and unusual wording. Do not guess or recreate the legal description. Include acreage if stated. Use Additional Information for short reference lines such as OUTSALE, EASEMENT, UTILITY ROW, C\&R, and LEASE when full indexing is not needed.

Names Searched goes last for familiarity. It should be built from the full review of the order, chain, mortgages, judgments, liens, probate documents, and other relevant documents, and should not be finalized until all relevant documents have been reviewed. List borrower(s) from the request form first, in the same order shown. Then include current owners, prior owners as needed, every grantor, every grantee, every person who obtained or owned the property, every person in the Chain of Title who bought or sold the property, parties relevant to judgments/liens when applicable, and heirs identified from wills, LOHs, and REAs. If a person is listed with AKA, FKA, FORMERLY KNOWN AS, maiden name, prior married name, remarried name, nickname, alias, or similar secondary-name language, preserve all known name variations in Names Searched. Even though Chain of Title entries should list names exactly as written in each deed, Names Searched should include the complete searchable name history found throughout the chain. Combine maiden names, prior married names, and remarried names into one full searchable name line when possible. Nicknames and aliases should be placed in parentheses. Do not include Special Commissioners or trustees on Trustee’s Deeds unless otherwise required. If a will, LOH, REA, scanned will, or reference to a will appears, include every person specifically identified as an heir in Names Searched. Add heirs only, not executors, administrators, witnesses, notaries, attorneys, commissioners, trustees, clerks, or affiants unless they are also specifically identified as heirs.

**1\. CORE ORGANIZATION RULE**

The final report does not have to match the exact visual style or formatting of the Hazelwood run sheet.

The important rule is that the information must be organized in a familiar workflow order so the report is easy to review, easy to compare against the source documents, easy to cut and paste from, and free of unnecessary clutter.

Use this general report section order:

1. ORDER INFORMATION  
2. CHAIN OF TITLE  
3. MORTGAGES / DEEDS OF TRUST  
4. JUDGMENTS / LIENS  
5. MISCELLANEOUS DOCUMENTS  
6. LEGAL DESCRIPTION  
7. ADDITIONAL INFORMATION  
8. NAMES SEARCHED

The output may use any clean, readable format as long as the information is grouped under the correct section.

The PDFs are usually compiled in this practical working order:

1. REQUEST SHEET / ORDER INFORMATION, IF PROVIDED  
2. ASSESSOR SHEETS, TAX INFORMATION, AND ORDER INFORMATION SUPPORTING DOCUMENTS  
3. DEEDS AND CHAIN OF TITLE DOCUMENTS, ALONG WITH PERTINENT SUPPORTING DOCUMENTS THAT APPEAR WITHIN THE CHAIN OF TITLE  
4. MORTGAGES / DEEDS OF TRUST, ALONG WITH ASSOCIATED MORTGAGE DOCUMENTS SUCH AS ASSIGNMENTS, MODIFICATIONS, AND SUBSTITUTE TRUSTEE DOCUMENTS

6. MISCELLANEOUS DOCUMENTS, SUCH AS OUTSALES, AGREEMENTS, LEASES, EASEMENTS, C\&RS, UTILITY ROWS, AND SIMILAR DOCUMENTS

Use the PDF compilation order to help identify what section each document belongs in, but place the extracted information into the correct report section.

Use ALL CAPS wherever practical for extracted report content, especially names, document titles, notes, short reference entries, and Names Searched entries.

11.7K

**2\. ORDER INFORMATION RULES**

**2.1 FILE NUMBER / ORDER NUMBER RULE**

For the File Number field on the order sheet, use the PDF filename first if it contains a usable order number and that number matches or corresponds with the order/request information.

If the PDF filename contains a number, use the entire number exactly as it appears.

Do not:

* drop digits  
* shorten the number  
* break apart number groups that belong together

Never invent an order number.

Examples:

512571-16683  
251113011

The request sheet, email, or first page may also confirm the order number and company name.

If the PDF filename and request sheet clearly show the same order number, use that order number for the File Number field.

If there is no usable order number assigned by the company, use the company name and full property address.

This may apply to companies such as:

EASTMAN CREDIT UNION  
CLEARCHOICE TITLE / CLEARCHOICE ABSTRACTING

Example:

EASTMAN CREDIT UNION 123 BIBLE LANE, BRISTOL, TN 37620

If there is no company name and no usable order number, use the full property address as the File Number.

---

**2.2 FINAL OUTPUT FILE NAME RULE**

For naming the final output file, use the company name plus the full ID when both are available.

Examples:

TACS 512571-16683  
MORTILES 251113011

If there is an ID but no company name, use the full ID alone.

Example:

512571-16683

If there is no usable ID but there is a company and property address, use the company name plus the full property address.

Example:

ECU 123 BIBLE LANE, BRISTOL, TN 37620

If there is no company name and no usable ID, use the full property address.

Never invent a file number, order number, or output filename ID.

---

**2.3 EFFECTIVE DATE RULE**

Always leave space for the Effective Date field.

Do not guess the Effective Date.

Do not automatically fill in an Effective Date unless it is clearly provided.

The Effective Date may be supplied by the user or written on the front page of the order.

Look for the Effective Date on the front page of the PDF. When present, it may appear as a large, bold date that is noticeably bigger than the surrounding print. If that large bold front-page date is clearly being used as the Effective Date, use it.

If the Effective Date is not clearly identified or clearly being used as the Effective Date, leave the field blank.

The Effective Date changes with each order because it depends on the last date the court records were updated for the requested search.

---

**2.4 COMPLETED DATE RULE**

Use the actual Completed Date when available.

If the Completed Date is not provided, leave it blank unless the user gives the date to use.

---

**2.5 PROPERTY ADDRESS RULE**

Use the full property address from the order or request documents.

Preserve the address as stated whenever possible.

---

**2.6 COUNTY RULE**

Use the county shown in the order, request sheet, tax record, deed, or other reliable source document.

---

**2.7 TOWNSHIP RULE**

Township should normally be the city from the property address.

Use the city exactly as it appears in the property address unless there is a rare reason not to.

Example:

PROPERTY ADDRESS: 1260 FAIRVIEW STREET, GRUNDY, VA 24614  
TOWNSHIP: GRUNDY

---

**2.8 PARCEL ID RULE**

Parcel IDs must be exact.

Do not drop leading zeroes.

If the document says 069A17, it must remain 069A17.

If there are multiple Parcel IDs, preserve them in the same order they appear in the source documents.

When multiple Parcel IDs appear, display them stacked vertically whenever possible instead of separated with commas.

---

**2.9 TAX INFORMATION RULE**

Capture all tax information exactly as it appears in the tax tickets and supporting documents.

Do not assume there are only first and second installments.

There may be more installments depending on the number of parcels and whether taxes are current or delinquent.

Capture every installment exactly as shown.

Extract and preserve, when present:

TAX ID  
TAX AMOUNT  
DUE  
DELINQUENT  
PAID  
ASSESSED VALUE  
LAND VALUE  
IMPROVEMENT VALUE

If there are multiple Parcel IDs, tax amounts, due dates, paid dates, or delinquent amounts, display them stacked one on top of the other instead of separated with commas whenever possible.

Keep them in the same order they appear in the documents.

---

**2.10 DELINQUENT TAX RULE**

Delinquent is not a yes/no field.

Delinquent must show the actual delinquent amount owed.

If a tax ticket is unpaid, capture:

ORIGINAL AMOUNT  
DUE DATE  
FULL DELINQUENT AMOUNT SHOWN ON THE TICKET, INCLUDING PENALTIES AND FEES IF LISTED

---

**2.11 CURRENT VESTING OWNER RULE**

List the current vesting owner as shown by the vesting deed or most current vesting instrument.

Use full names.

Do not state marital status.

If the instrument specifically shows the parties as married, such as husband and wife, join their names with an ampersand.

Example:

JOHN SMITH & JANE SMITH

If the instrument does not specifically show them as married, separate their names with commas.

Example:

JOHN SMITH, JANE SMITH

Always use both full names.

Follow the wording of each specific instrument.

---

**2.12 SEARCH DEPTH RULE**

Do not display search depth on the final report.

Search depth is internal request-sheet information only.

---

**2.13 EXCISE TAX RULE**

Excise tax may remain on the report if available, but it is not required.

Do not treat excise tax as a necessary field.

**3\. CHAIN OF TITLE RULES**

**3.1 CHAIN OF TITLE ORDER RULE**

Chain of Title entries must be listed from newest to oldest.

The newest applicable deed or conveyance should appear first.

Older deeds or conveyances should follow in reverse chronological order.

Chain of Title entries must be separate numbered entries.

Number chain entries in order:

1, 2, 3, 4, etc.

Do not repeat number 1 for every chain entry.

Each actual deed or conveyance should receive its own numbered Chain of Title entry unless a special rule says otherwise.

Outsales are usually not fully indexed as numbered Chain of Title entries. They are usually listed in Additional Information.

---

**3.2 CHAIN ENTRY FORMAT RULE**

For each numbered Chain of Title entry, capture:

DOCUMENT TITLE  
BOOK / INSTRUMENT  
PAGE  
DATED  
RECORDED  
CONSIDERATION  
IN / OUT SALE?  
GRANTOR(S)  
GRANTEE(S)

The report does not have to visually match a specific template, but these fields should be included when available.

---

**3.3 CHAIN ENTRY COMPLETENESS RULE**

For every numbered Chain of Title entry:

* list all Grantor(s)  
* list all Grantee(s)  
* keep names in the proper place even if the same person appears on both sides  
* preserve life estate language when applicable  
* preserve special wording such as REMAINDERMENT, RESERVES LIFE ESTATE, RESERVE LIFE ESTATE, or similar wording reflected by the instrument  
* preserve recorded name qualifiers exactly as written, including AKA, FKA, FORMERLY KNOWN AS, and similar language

---

**3.4 DEED TITLE RULE**

Use simplified deed titles that are short, familiar, and consistent.

Examples include:

GENERAL WARRANTY  
SPECIAL WARRANTY  
QUITCLAIM  
GIFT DEED  
DEED OF ASSUMPTION  
ESCHEAT DEED  
PARTITION DEED  
TRUSTEE’S DEED

If a deed type ends in DEED, simplify it when appropriate, but preserve the correct deed meaning.

Additional deed titles may be added later using the same short-form style.

---

**3.5 CONSIDERATION RULE**

Consideration must come from the deed itself.

Do not use consideration from the title sheet, cover page, request sheet, or summary page if it differs from the deed.

If the deed says $1.00, use $1.00.

Only enter consideration if the deed specifically gives:

A NUMERIC DOLLAR AMOUNT  
or  
LOVE AND AFFECTION

Examples:

11,700.00  
900.00  
$1.00  
LOVE AND AFFECTION

If the deed does not specifically state a numeric amount or LOVE AND AFFECTION, leave Consideration blank.

Do not guess or summarize other types of consideration.

Show full numeric values with complete digits and proper comma placement.

Do not abbreviate numeric amounts.

---

**3.6 IN / OUT SALE RULE**

Show In/Out Sale as a Yes/No checkbox or equivalent Yes/No field.

Use:

☒ YES ☐ NO

or

☐ YES ☒ NO

For Chain of Title, treat an insale as an instrument in which the current vesting owner receives some or all of the target property.

A current vesting owner may acquire the property through multiple purchases over time, sometimes a tract at a time, so there may be more than one insale.

An outsale is any time the target property, or a piece of the target property, is sold off by the current owner or by a prior owner.

Mark YES when the current vesting owner either:

TAKES IN SOME OF THE LAND  
or  
SELLS OUT SOME OF THE LAND

For the In/Out Sale box in the Chain of Title, this will almost always be marked for insales.

Outsales are usually not checked in the numbered Chain of Title entries. They are usually listed separately in Additional Information.

---

**3.7 GRANTOR / GRANTEE DUPLICATE NAME RULE**

If a person appears as both a grantor and a grantee in the same instrument, list that name in both places.

Do not simplify it away.

If the instrument shows the same person on both sides, the report must also show that same person on both sides.

---

**3.8 MARITAL STATUS / NAME FORMAT RULE**

Do not use a separate marital-status field.

Do not state marital status.

If two parties are listed in the instrument as married spouses, separate their names with an ampersand.

Example:

JOHN SMITH & JANE SMITH

If they are not listed as married in the instrument, separate their names with commas.

Example:

JOHN SMITH, JANE SMITH

Always use the full name of both spouses or both parties.

Do not shorten or omit one spouse’s name just because they are married.

If one instrument lists them as married and another instrument does not, follow the wording of that specific instrument.

---

**3.9 EXACT RECORDED NAME AND LANGUAGE RULE**

Grantors and grantees must be listed exactly as written in the deed.

Preserve AKA, FKA, FORMERLY KNOWN AS, and similar recorded name language.

Preserve all recorded wording exactly, including misspellings, punctuation, abbreviations, and apparent errors if that is how the instrument is recorded.

Descriptions, names, and other deed language must be indexed exactly as written in the recorded instrument.

This includes:

SPELLING  
PUNCTUATION  
ABBREVIATIONS  
FORMATTING DETAILS  
APPARENT MISTAKES IN THE RECORDED DOCUMENT

Even if the recorded wording appears incorrect, index it exactly as recorded.

Do not silently correct deed language, names, descriptions, spelling, punctuation, abbreviations, or apparent recording mistakes.

---

**3.10 LIFE ESTATE RULE**

If a deed states that a grantor, or one or more grantors, reserves a life estate, the Grantee(s) line must show that grantor or those grantors as reserving the life estate, and the other grantee or grantees as REMAINDERMENT.

Use this format:

\[NAME\] RESERVES LIFE ESTATE, \[NAME\] REMAINDERMENT

If more than one grantor reserves the life estate, list those grantors first, followed by the person or persons receiving the remainderment.

Examples:

PAMELA J. FLEMING RESERVES LIFE ESTATE, GEORGE THOMAS GRINDSTAFF REMAINDERMENT

JOHN SMITH & JANE SMITH RESERVE LIFE ESTATE, DAVID SMITH REMAINDERMENT

JOHN SMITH RESERVES LIFE ESTATE, DAVID SMITH & MARY SMITH REMAINDERMENT

If the deed language makes clear that the grantor or grantors keep the life estate and another person or persons take the property after their death, the Grantee(s) line must reflect that distinction instead of listing all parties as ordinary grantees.

---

**3.11 DESCRIPTION / NOTES RULE**

Do not give a description or extra note for every deed.

Only the vesting deed generally needs the full property description.

For other instruments in the Chain of Title, no notes are needed unless there is something special that should be called out.

Only add notes when something special is involved, such as:

DECEASED PERSON REFERENCES  
WILL REFERENCES  
LOHS  
REAS  
LIFE ESTATE LANGUAGE  
DIVORCE REFERENCES  
THIRD-PARTY OR FOURTH-PARTY ISSUES  
TRUSTEE’S DEED / FORECLOSURE-RELATED ITEMS  
OTHER SPECIAL SUPPORTING DOCUMENTS

Do not repeat deed descriptions for every instrument unless there is a special reason.

When notes are used, list them in the same order they appear in the source documents.

---

**3.12 SPECIAL NOTE HANDLING RULE**

All probate, deceased-person, will, LOH, REA, divorce, and similar special-note rules apply.

Use a Yes/No field for the special situation when the report structure provides one.

If the answer is YES, insert the applicable note information into the corresponding field.

Examples of note wording:

WILL OF \[NAME\] \[BOOK/PAGE\]

LOH FOR \[NAME\] \[BOOK/PAGE\]

REA FOR \[NAME\] \[BOOK/PAGE OR CASE REFERENCE\]

NO WILL OR LOH FOR \[NAME\] \[DATE OF DEATH IF KNOWN\]

REFERENCE MADE TO A WILL FOR \[NAME\] BUT NONE WAS FOUND

---

**3.13 DECEASED PERSON / PROBATE ANNOTATION RULE**

If a chain entry involves a deceased person, add the applicable note directly with that chain entry or in the corresponding special-note field.

Use the following wording when applicable:

WILL OF \[DECEASED PERSON\] \[BOOK/PAGE\]

LOH FOR \[DECEASED PERSON\] \[BOOK/PAGE\]

REA FOR \[DECEASED PERSON\] \[BOOK/PAGE OR CASE REFERENCE\]

NO WILL OR LOH FOR \[DECEASED PERSON\] \[DATE OF DEATH IF KNOWN\]

REFERENCE MADE TO A WILL FOR \[DECEASED PERSON\] BUT NONE WAS FOUND

These notes are not separate deed entries.

They should appear with the applicable chain entry or special-note field.

---

**3.14 TRUSTEE’S DEED / FORECLOSURE RULE**

If a Trustee’s Deed appears in the Chain of Title, treat the Trustee’s Deed itself as the numbered chain entry.

After the Trustee’s Deed is indexed, list related foreclosure documents under the special-note or related-document handling for that entry rather than as separate numbered chain entries.

Related foreclosure documents may include:

ACCOUNT OF SALE  
SUBSTITUTE TRUSTEES  
MODIFICATION  
FORECLOSED DOT  
OTHER DIRECTLY RELATED FORECLOSURE DOCUMENTS

If more than one DOT appears under a Trustee’s Deed, identify which DOT is the one actually referenced in the Trustee’s Deed as the foreclosed DOT.

If it is the DOT being foreclosed, list it as:

FORECLOSED DOT \[BOOK/PAGE OR INSTRUMENT\]

If it is a different DOT that was still open at the time of foreclosure, list it as:

DOT OPEN AT THE TIME OF FORECLOSURE \[BOOK/PAGE OR INSTRUMENT\]

These foreclosure-related documents are supporting reference documents only.

They are not separate numbered Chain of Title entries.

When foreclosure-related documents appear under or around a Trustee’s Deed, keep them in the same order they appear in the packet.

Resume numbered Chain of Title entries when the next actual deed or conveyance appears.

---

**3.15 OLD DEED RECORDING DATE RULE**

On very old deeds, look for the word TESTE.

The recording date is often found in the final paragraph immediately before the TESTE line and before the clerk’s signature.

When indexing very old deeds, examine that final paragraph carefully to locate the recording date.

---

**4\. MORTGAGES / DEEDS OF TRUST RULES**

**4.1 MORTGAGE SECTION ORDER RULE**

Open or reportable Mortgages / Deeds of Trust should be listed from oldest to newest.

The oldest open or reportable mortgage or deed of trust should appear first.

Newer open or reportable mortgages or deeds of trust should follow in chronological order.

Associated mortgage documents should be listed under the mortgage they relate to whenever possible.

---

**4.2 MORTGAGE SECTION FORMAT RULE**

Place Mortgages / Deeds of Trust after Chain of Title.

Capture these fields when available:

OPEN MORTGAGES / DEEDS OF TRUST TO REPORT?  
DOCUMENT TITLE  
BOOK / INSTRUMENT  
PAGE  
DATED  
RECORDED  
CONSIDERATION  
MATURITY DATE  
LENDER  
BORROWER  
TRUSTEE  
ASSOCIATED DOCUMENTS  
OPEN / CLOSED ENDED?

The report does not have to visually match a specific template, but these fields should be included when available.

---

**4.3 MORTGAGE DOCUMENT TYPE RULE**

Common mortgage document titles should use short-form abbreviations when appropriate.

Common examples:

DOT \= DEED OF TRUST  
RFDT \= REFINANCE DEED OF TRUST  
DTCL \= CREDIT LINE DEED OF TRUST

Use these short forms unless the instrument clearly requires another title.

---

**4.4 ASSOCIATED MORTGAGE DOCUMENTS RULE**

Associated mortgage documents are usually:

ASSIGNMENT  
SUBSTITUTE TRUSTEE  
MODIFICATION

Always allow OTHER as an option so a custom associated-document title can be entered if needed.

Associated mortgage documents should be listed under the mortgage they relate to whenever possible.

---

**4.5 OPEN-ENDED / CLOSED-ENDED RULE**

For mortgage documents, identify whether the loan is open-ended or closed-ended when that information is available.

OPEN-ENDED means the borrower may continue to borrow additional money while paying down the loan balance.

Look for language such as:

FUTURE ADVANCE  
FUTURE ADVANCEMENT  
OPEN-ENDED  
SIMILAR WORDING

CLOSED-ENDED means the loan is for one stated amount advanced one time and then repaid over time.

A maturity date is common on closed-ended loans, but it may also appear on some open-ended loans.

Do not rely on maturity date alone to determine whether a loan is open-ended or closed-ended.

When the mortgage section includes an Open/Closed Ended field, fill it according to the instrument language.

Use:

OPEN-ENDED  
or  
CLOSED-ENDED

---

**5\. JUDGMENTS / LIENS RULES**

**5.1 JUDGMENTS / LIENS SECTION RULE**

Place Judgments / Liens after Mortgages / Deeds of Trust.

Capture these fields when available:

DOCUMENT TITLE  
BOOK / INSTRUMENT  
PAGE  
DATED  
RECORDED  
CASE \#  
AMOUNT  
PLAINTIFF  
DEFENDANT

Parties relevant to judgments and liens should also be considered for Names Searched when applicable.

If no judgments or liens are found, use:

NO JUDGMENTS OR LIENS FOUND IN THE RECORD.

**6\. MISCELLANEOUS DOCUMENTS RULES**

**6.1 MISCELLANEOUS DOCUMENTS SECTION RULE**

Place Miscellaneous Documents after Judgments / Liens.

Use this section for remaining supporting recorded documents that affect the property and need fuller detail.

These may include:

**7\. LEGAL DESCRIPTION RULES**

**7.1 LEGAL DESCRIPTION SECTION RULE**

Place Legal Description after Miscellaneous Documents for familiarity.

The legal description should usually be pulled from the vesting deed because that is normally the best source.

Use the vesting deed legal description when it is readable.

If the vesting deed legal description is not readable, incomplete, or unclear, pull the legal description from another reliable recorded source.

Acceptable backup sources may include:

A PRIOR DEED  
A LATER DEED  
A DEED OF TRUST / MORTGAGE WITH THE LEGAL DESCRIPTION ATTACHED  
ANOTHER RECORDED INSTRUMENT THAT CLEARLY CONTAINS THE PROPERTY DESCRIPTION

A mortgage or deed of trust may contain the legal description as an attachment, often on the last page.

Use the best readable legal description available.

Also include acreage if stated.

Do not guess or recreate the legal description.

Do not repeat full deed descriptions for every Chain of Title entry unless there is a special reason.

If multiple sources contain the legal description, prefer the vesting deed first when readable.

The legal description must be recorded exactly as written in the source deed or other recorded instrument being used.

Preserve all wording exactly, including:

MISSPELLINGS  
ABBREVIATIONS  
PUNCTUATION  
CAPITALIZATION DIFFERENCES  
UNUSUAL PHRASING

Even if the description appears wrong, incomplete, or awkward, reproduce it exactly as recorded in the source being indexed.

Do not silently correct legal description language.

**8\. ADDITIONAL INFORMATION RULES**

**8.1 ADDITIONAL INFORMATION SECTION RULE**

Place Additional Information after Legal Description for familiarity.

Additional Information is usually used for shorthand references when full indexing is not necessary.

Miscellaneous Documents and Additional Information are somewhat interchangeable depending on how much detail is needed.

Additional Information is usually preferred for short shorthand references.

Miscellaneous Documents should be used when a document needs fuller indexing or fuller detail.

Outsales are usually listed in Additional Information instead of being fully indexed as numbered Chain of Title entries.

Documents that may appear in this part of the PDF include:

OUTSALES  
ADDITIONAL DEEDS  
AGREEMENTS  
LEASE AGREEMENTS  
EASEMENTS  
UTILITY RIGHT-OF-WAY DOCUMENTS  
COVENANTS AND RESTRICTIONS / C\&R / C\&RS  
SURVEYS / PLATS  
OTHER RECORDED DOCUMENTS AFFECTING THE PROPERTY

When using Additional Information for outsales or other supporting instruments, list:

BOOK / INSTRUMENT AND PAGE REFERENCE  
followed by  
SHORT DOCUMENT LABEL

Examples:

2016/6754 OUTSALE  
738/1035 EASEMENT  
736/743 UTILITY ROW  
725/210 C\&R

Use ALL CAPS.

If the item needs fuller indexing, place it in Miscellaneous Documents.

Use judgment based on the importance and detail of the document.

**9\. NAMES SEARCHED RULES**

**9.1 NAMES SEARCHED SECTION RULE**

Place Names Searched at the bottom of the report for familiarity.

This placement is for convenience and familiarity, not because Names Searched necessarily appears at the end of the PDF.

Names Searched should be built from the full review of the order, chain, mortgages, judgments, liens, probate documents, and other relevant documents.

Names Searched should not be finalized until all relevant documents have been reviewed.

If more than one borrower is listed on the request form, list those borrowers first in the same order they appear on the request form.

After the borrower or borrowers, list the remaining Names Searched entries as applicable.

Names Searched must include:

BORROWER(S) LISTED ON THE REQUEST FORM FIRST  
CURRENT OWNERS  
PRIOR OWNERS AS NEEDED  
EVERY GRANTOR  
EVERY GRANTEE  
EVERY PERSON WHO OBTAINED OR OWNED THE PROPERTY  
EVERY PERSON IN THE CHAIN OF TITLE WHO BOUGHT OR SOLD THE PROPERTY  
PARTIES RELEVANT TO JUDGMENTS / LIENS WHEN APPLICABLE  
HEIRS IDENTIFIED FROM WILLS, LOHS, AND REAS

In general, anybody listed as a grantor or grantee should be included in Names Searched.

If a person is listed in a deed with an AKA, FKA, FORMERLY KNOWN AS, maiden name, prior married name, remarried name, nickname, alias, or other secondary name, preserve all known name variations in Names Searched.

Even though each Chain of Title entry should list the person exactly as written in that specific deed, the Names Searched section should preserve the complete searchable name history for that person when available.

If the same person appears under multiple names throughout the Chain of Title, include all known name variations in Names Searched.

This includes name changes caused by marriage, remarriage, divorce, maiden names, prior married names, AKA, FKA, FORMERLY KNOWN AS, nicknames, aliases, or similar recorded name changes.

Use the primary or most complete current name first.

Combine maiden names, prior married names, and remarried names into one full searchable name line when possible.

Nicknames and aliases should be placed in parentheses.

Examples:

JOHN (JOHNNY) SMITH

MARY JONES BROWN TAYLOR SMITH

SARAH (SALLY) ANN CARTER WILSON

If a woman appears under multiple married names and/or a maiden name throughout the chain, include all names found in the record in Names Searched.

Do not lose earlier names just because the current deed uses a later married name.

Exceptions:

SPECIAL COMMISSIONERS DO NOT NEED TO BE INCLUDED IN NAMES SEARCHED.

TRUSTEES ON TRUSTEE’S DEEDS DO NOT NEED TO BE INCLUDED IN NAMES SEARCHED.

---

**9.2 HEIRS IN NAMES SEARCHED RULE**

If a will, reference to a will, scanned will, List of Heirs, LOH, or Real Estate Affidavit / REA appears in the PDF, identify every person named as an heir in that document and make sure each heir is included in Names Searched.

This rule applies to heirs only.

Do not add a person to Names Searched just because they appear in the will, LOH, or REA unless they are specifically identified as an heir.

Do not automatically add:

EXECUTORS  
ADMINISTRATORS  
WITNESSES  
NOTARIES  
ATTORNEYS  
COMMISSIONERS  
TRUSTEES  
CLERKS  
AFFIANTS

unless that person is also specifically identified as an heir.

If a will, LOH, or REA is found, every heir named in that document must be included in Names Searched.

Heirs only.
